Sealcoating & crack filling

What's the difference between crack filling and sealcoating?

Crack filling seals individual cracks so water can't get into the base. Sealcoating is a protective coat over the whole surface that shields it from sun and water and restores the black finish. Most pavement does best with both: cracks filled first, then sealcoat.

How often should asphalt be sealcoated?

As a general guideline, every 2–3 years, with crack filling as needed in between. Heavy traffic and full sun mean more often; shaded, lightly used surfaces can go longer. We'll tell you honestly what yours needs.

How long before I can drive on it?

It depends on the product, the temperature and the humidity. We'll tell you exactly how long to keep traffic off your surface before we leave.

Will sealcoat fix my potholes?

No. Sealcoat protects a surface that's in reasonable shape; potholes and broken areas need to be repaired first. See our driveway repair and parking lot repair pages.

Scheduling & weather

Why would you reschedule my job because of the weather?

Sealcoat and crack sealant need dry weather and the right temperatures to cure properly. If it's cold or drizzling, we'll call you ahead of time and pick a better day rather than do the job poorly.

Can you work around my business hours?

Yes. For parking lots we plan the work around your operating schedule to keep disruption to your customers as low as possible.

What if I notice a problem after the job?

Let us know and we'll come back and take a look. We also check in after the job to ask how it's holding up.
